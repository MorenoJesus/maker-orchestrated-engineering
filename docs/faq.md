# Frequently asked questions

## Is MOE replacing Agile, Scrum, or Kanban?
No. It adds explicit human-agent responsibilities, authority, and evidence. Teams retain their existing lifecycle unless they independently choose to change it.

## Must we use several agents?
No. A human with one approved agent can use MOE. More agents are useful only when the work is separable and review capacity is sufficient.

## Is the maker a manager instead of an engineer?
Not necessarily. The maker remains technically accountable and may write code, debug, design, and test. The role emphasizes ownership, not distance from implementation.

## Are five contracts too much paperwork?
They are five sets of semantics, not necessarily five files. Reuse existing work items and automation. Record only what the risk and policy require, but do not omit accountability or evidence.

## Do we need a special tool protocol or model?
No. The framework specifies boundaries and outcomes, not transport protocols or vendors. Native functions, approved APIs, command wrappers, or other mechanisms can implement the same contract.

## Does a clean scan or passing test suite prove safety?
No. It provides evidence within a tool's scope and configuration. Review its limitations, applicability, and candidate binding.

## Does MOE require human approval for every action?
No. Organizations may preauthorize bounded, low-risk actions under enforceable policy. Human accountability remains, and agents cannot expand their own authority.

## Is test-driven development mandatory?
No. It is a useful optional skill. If a team claims test-first execution, it should retain meaningful red/green evidence. Other verification approaches must meet the mission's needs.

## Is MOE already a validated industry standard?
No. Version 0.3.0 is a proposed working draft. Pilots must test whether it improves delivery without weakening quality or increasing unsustainable review effort.

## Can we use our own requirements style?
Yes. Any style that preserves intent, observable acceptance, explicit uncertainty, and traceability can fit. ECS is the recommended reference and the MOE repository's own writing standard, but no separate writing standard is mandatory for adopters. See [ECS integration](../implementation/ecs-integration.md).

## Can we reuse the public repository today?
The owner still needs to select and approve a license. Public visibility alone is not the project's declaration of an open-source license. See the license status document.
